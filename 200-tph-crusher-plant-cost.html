200 TPH Enterprise Crusher Plant Cost & Configuration Guide

Comprehensive B2B cost analysis, layout configurations, power requirements, and complete project costs for an enterprise-level 200 TPH crushing plant.

Published: May 2026 | SREW Projects Precision Engineers

A 200 TPH (Tons Per Hour) stone crushing plant represents a full-scale industrial aggregate manufacturing enterprise. Operating at this scale is highly profitable, allowing operators to secure bulk supply contracts for high-speed expressways, large-scale metropolitan housing construction, and commercial concrete manufacturing. Achieving a reliable 200 TPH output requires state-of-the-art machinery, hydraulic automation, and heavy structural engineering. This guide details SREW Projects' turnkey 200 TPH CAPEX breakdown and technical specifications.

1. Enterprise-Grade 200 TPH Equipment Blueprint

A continuous 200 TPH output demands high-amplitude machinery and robust feed capacities. The optimal three-stage crushing circuit designed by SREW Projects includes:

  • Primary Station: Heavy-Duty GF-1200 x 4500 Grizzly Feeder feeding an SREW Projects JS-3624 Primary Jaw Crusher (accepts feed size up to 650mm).
  • Secondary Station: SREW Projects High Performance Multi-Cylinder Hydraulic Cone Crusher Model MC-250 (185 kW) for reliable size reduction.
  • Tertiary Station (Optional for Sand): SREW Projects VSI M-Sand Machine (VSI-9000) for precise aggregate shaping and fine sand production.
  • Screening Station: SREW Projects 4-Deck Heavy Vibrating Screen (VS-2060) classifying aggregates with extreme precision.
  • Logistics Network: Heavy channel belt conveyor network (widths 1000mm and 1200mm).

Frequently Asked Questions

A 200 TPH two-stage primary-secondary crushing plant has a total connected electrical load of approximately 420 kW to 460 kW. We recommend installing a dedicated 630 kVA transformer or double DG set loops.

At 200 TPH, a plant produces 2000 tons of material in a standard 10-hour shift. This requires handling approximately 100 to 120 heavy tipper trucks daily, demanding spacious logistics planning and broad internal roadways.
